# Move employee advances out of an opening prepaid schedule into per-person Employee Advances

Some employee advances were entered inside the opening prepaid allocation, so they sit in Prepaid expenses (1450) and are amortised as an expense. [SCRUM-961](https://autoflowdealer.atlassian.net/browse/SCRUM-961). The new command moves a stated amount per person out of one posted opening prepaid schedule into one Employee Advances row each, so payroll can recover them. The amounts, the people and the reason are inputs. Nothing in the code depends on one organisation. The production case (620.000 JOD, two people) lives only in the test fixture.

Decision (owner, Option A). One reviewed correction per schedule. A preparer with finance and payroll authority requests it. A second person with the same authority approves it. The requester and the approver must differ, and neither may be a beneficiary. A pending request can be dismissed. The approval re-reads the schedule and refuses if the figures the approver saw have changed.

The journal is one ADJUSTMENT entry. Debit Employee Advances (1230) for the moved total. Credit Prepaid expenses for the moved total less the true-up. Credit the schedule's expense account for the true-up. Cash, banks, receivables, payables and equity are untouched. The posted opening journal, its lines and the draft's allocations are never edited. The schedule's total falls by the moved amount and its recognised figure falls to what the cron's own rule gives on the new total for the months already recognised. The true-up is the difference, and it is returned to expense because those months were over-expensed.

Worked case. Total 7,748.000 over 6 months, one month recognised (1,291.333). Moving 620.000 gives a new total of 7,128.000 and a recognised figure of 1,188.000. The true-up is 103.333. The journal is Dr Employee Advances 620.000, Cr Prepaid 516.667, Cr General Expense 103.333. The next month recognises 1,188.000. Prepaid reconciles with no shortfall and Employee Advances reconcile at 620.000.

The true-up uses the cron's iterative rule (`floor(remaining / months left)`, last month takes the rest), not a proportional largest-remainder rule. The shortfall reader for a schedule (`recognizedDueThroughDateMinor`) projects the same iterative rule from the schedule's stored progress, so the cron, the shortfall reader and the reconciliation agree by construction; only the expense-document fallback (`computeAmortizationInfo`) uses largest-remainder, and it is not used for scheduled prepaid. A property test (terms 1..12, every months-recognised count, non-dividing totals) pins the agreement after approval and after every later month. A test row with a carried remainder (term 4, three months) fails if the rule is replaced by a proportional one.

Proof. The event type `PREPAID_RECLASSIFIED_TO_ADVANCES` is posted through the accounting engine with a hook proof that re-derives the amount and true-up from the stored request and schedule, so no internal caller can post other figures. The prepaid source resolver accepts a schedule below its posted allocation only when exactly one approved reclassification is proven by its event, journal and debit to Employee Advances. The opening advance basis decides a reclassified advance before the ordinary shortcut, and checks the marker against the request in both directions, so removing the marker cannot turn it into an ordinary advance. Both orderings are mutation-proved (logs in the SCRUM-961 report).

Not built, and recorded as an obligation. There is no reversal door in version 1. Generic reversal of this event is refused with `PREPAID_RECLASS_REVERSAL`. A mistake is corrected by a new reviewed change. A future reversal (packet invariant I17) must undo, in one transaction: the journal through a linked reversal; every advance row, and only when its recovered amount is zero, it has no recovery rows and is not deleted; the schedule's total and recognised figure; and the request state. It is allowed only while no later recognition and no recovery exists. It needs its own owner ruling and its own tests before it is enabled.

Known limits. The journal is dated at approval, so the true-up lands in the approval period, not in the earlier month that was over-expensed; a closed approval day refuses. The amortisation outbox is not read; an inconsistent schedule is refused through the cron-history check instead. Concurrency (two approvals at once) relies on Convex serialisable transactions and is not provable in the test harness. The reconciliation of recognised-to-date for non-dividing totals is exact only under the cron's rule.

Production roster at the time of design: three eligible non-beneficiary members, all with the OWNER role, so a two-person approval is possible without adding anyone.

To roll back before approval, dismiss the request. After approval there is no automatic rollback in version 1.
