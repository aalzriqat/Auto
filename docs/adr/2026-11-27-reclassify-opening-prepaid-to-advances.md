# Move employee advances out of an opening prepaid schedule into per-person Employee Advances

Some employee advances were entered inside the opening prepaid allocation, so they sit in Prepaid expenses (1450) and are amortised as an expense. [SCRUM-961](https://autoflowdealer.atlassian.net/browse/SCRUM-961). The new command moves a stated amount per person out of one posted opening prepaid schedule into one Employee Advances row each, so payroll can recover them. The amounts, the people and the reason are inputs. Nothing in the code depends on one organisation. The production case (620.000 JOD, two people) lives only in the test fixture.

Decision (owner, Option A). One reviewed correction per schedule. A preparer with finance and payroll authority requests it. A second person with the same authority approves it. The requester and the approver must differ, and neither may be a beneficiary. A pending request can be dismissed by its requester or by any other authorised non-beneficiary; a support (impersonation) session and a beneficiary are refused. The approval re-reads the schedule and refuses if the figures the approver saw have changed.

The journal is one ADJUSTMENT entry of three lines (two when the true-up is 0). Debit Employee Advances (1230) for the moved total as one line. Credit Prepaid expenses for the moved total less the true-up. Credit the schedule's expense account for the true-up. The per-person split lives on the advance rows. Cash, banks, receivables, payables and equity are untouched. The posted opening journal, its lines and the draft's allocations are never edited. The schedule's total falls by the moved amount and its recognised figure falls to what the cron's own rule gives on the new total for the months already recognised. The true-up is the difference, and it is returned to expense because those months were over-expensed.

Worked case. Total 7,748.000 over 6 months, one month recognised (1,291.333). Moving 620.000 gives a new total of 7,128.000 and a recognised figure of 1,188.000. The true-up is 103.333. The journal is Dr Employee Advances 620.000, Cr Prepaid 516.667, Cr General Expense 103.333. The next month recognises 1,188.000. Prepaid reconciles with no shortfall and Employee Advances reconcile at 620.000.

The true-up uses the cron's iterative rule (`floor(remaining / months left)`, last month takes the rest), not a proportional largest-remainder rule. The shortfall reader for a schedule (`recognizedDueThroughDateMinor`) projects the same iterative rule from the schedule's stored progress, so the cron, the shortfall reader and the reconciliation agree by construction; only the expense-document fallback (`computeAmortizationInfo`) uses largest-remainder, and it is not used for scheduled prepaid. A property test (terms 1..12, every months-recognised count, non-dividing totals) pins the agreement after approval and after every later month. In the numeric table of `convex/openingPrepaidReclassification.scrum961.test.ts` the term-4 row (total 1_100_002, three months recognised) is meant to catch a proportional truncation of the true-up, and the term-12 row (total 1_000_003, three months recognised) is meant to catch replacing the cron rule with largest remainder. Mutation runs on the implementation, with logs kept: a proportional true-up rule (killed by the term-4 row), no true-up at all, and the ORDINARY-before-OPENING ordering (each killed by a test). A largest-remainder mutant was run once against the cron-versus-shortfall agreement property test and survived it, which is why the numeric rows exist; no log of that run was kept, and no largest-remainder mutant was run against the term-12 row, so that row is not proven to catch it.

Proof. The event type `PREPAID_RECLASSIFIED_TO_ADVANCES` is posted through the accounting engine with a hook proof that re-derives the amount and true-up from the stored request and schedule, so no internal caller can post other figures. The prepaid source resolver accepts a schedule below its posted allocation only when exactly one approved reclassification is proven by its event, journal and debit to Employee Advances. The opening advance basis decides a reclassified advance before the ordinary shortcut, and checks the marker against the request in both directions, so removing the marker cannot turn it into an ordinary advance.

Not built, and recorded as an obligation. There is no reversal door in version 1. Generic reversal of this event is refused with `PREPAID_RECLASS_REVERSAL`. A mistake is corrected by a new reviewed change. A future reversal (packet invariant I17) must undo, in one transaction: the journal through a linked reversal; every advance row, and only when its recovered amount is zero, it has no recovery rows and is not deleted; the schedule's total and recognised figure; and the request state. It is allowed only while no later recognition and no recovery exists. It needs its own owner ruling and its own tests before it is enabled.

Readers agree with the ledger. The true-up is a negative recognition in the approval month. One shared loader (`convex/utils/prepaidRecognitionEvents.ts`) supplies it to the operational expense reports and to `listSchedules`, so every reader of recognised prepaid expense equals the GL after approval. Decision: one loader, not a patch in each report, so a later reader cannot forget the true-up. The true-up loader (`loadPostedReclassTrueUps`) is bounded at 500 and throws when the bound is reached. `postedAmortizationByScheduleId` also reads all posted amortisation events with an unbounded `.collect()` that it inherited from `listSchedules` on main; this change did not bound it.

Advance basis lookup. `reclassifiedAdvanceBasis` follows the advance's own marker to its request and verifies it in both directions. An unmarked advance is checked only against the org's APPROVED requests (index `by_org_status`, bounded, fail closed), so pending and dismissed history never affects an ordinary advance.

Obligation on later work: no advance delete or write-off door exists in `convex/` on main (searched for `isDeleted: true` and `WRITTEN_OFF` writers on 2026-11-27). Advances created here are recovered only through `recoverAdvance` and payroll. A write-off or delete door added later must handle rows that carry `prepaidReclassificationId`.

Visibility. Request, approve and dismiss audit rows use the `PAYROLL_MANAGER` read policy, so a reader with only VIEW_PAYROLL does not see the reason or lines.

Catch-up. `catchUpPrepaidSchedule` is the cron and manual path and may recognise several missed months after a reclassification, one event per month. That is harmless: each event posts its own month and the true-up is subtracted once. A single event covering several months is not reachable; a request for a schedule in that shape is refused as unsettled (`PREPAID_RECLASS_RECOGNITION`), and a test pins that at request time.

Edge case. A true-up that is not smaller than the moved total is refused as `PREPAID_RECLASS_INELIGIBLE` on purpose, so the credit to prepaid stays positive.

Known limits. The journal is dated at approval, so the true-up lands in the approval period, not in the earlier month that was over-expensed; a closed approval day refuses. The amortisation outbox is read: a non-POSTED outbox row, an open amortisation failure or a non-POSTED amortisation event for the schedule refuses the request as `PREPAID_RECLASS_RECOGNITION`. The command does not itself check for an org reset or opening cleanup in progress, and no test drives a request during one. A reset requires the organization to be suspended, and a suspended organization is refused by `requireTenantAuth` and the posting engine. An opening void refuses while any accounting event or queued outbox row exists, which an approved reclassification creates, and counts any request row as a reference. The new table and the advance marker are also declared in the reset and deletion registries. Approving twice is refused with `PREPAID_RECLASS_STATE`. Concurrency (two approvals at once) relies on Convex serialisable transactions and is not provable in the test harness. The reconciliation of recognised-to-date for non-dividing totals is exact only under the cron's rule.

Production roster: a design-time reading found three eligible non-beneficiary members sharing one role; it was not re-verified for this change.

To roll back before approval, dismiss the request. After approval there is no automatic rollback in version 1.
